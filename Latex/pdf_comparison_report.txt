1.1 Overview of 12-Lead Electrocardiography and
Clinical Significance
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), encompassing coronary artery disease, heart failure,
and diverse cardiac arrhythmias, represent the paramount contributor to human mor-
tality worldwide. According to oﬀicial global epidemiological assessments released by
the World Health Organization (WHO), cardiovascular pathologies were accountable
for approximately 17.9 million deaths in 2019 alone, representing nearly 32% of all
global deaths [1, 2]. Among non-communicable conditions, acute and chronic cardiac
illnesses maintain the highest global disease burden. Alarmingly, greater than 75%
of these deaths occur in low- and middle-income nations, where access to specialized
cardiological healthcare infrastructure is severely constrained and the ratio of certified
expert cardiologists to patients is strikingly deficient [1].
Theelectrocardiogram(ECG)remainsthegoldstandard,non-invasive,cost-effective,
and universally deployed clinical modality for recording the rhythmic electrical poten-
tials of the human myocardium [3]. During each heartbeat, an organized wave of
electrical depolarization and repolarization traverses cardiac myocytes, generating bio-
electric vector fields that propagate through body tissues to the surface of the skin.
Anelectrocardiographcapturesthesemicroscopicpotentialdifferentialsviaanarrange-
ment of surface electrodes placed strategically on the patient’s upper extremities, lower
extremities, and thorax.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Overview
Phishing continues to stand as one of the most prolific, damaging, and rapidly evolv-
ing vectors in modern cybersecurity. In a classic phishing attack, malicious adversaries
engineer deceptive electronic communications, websites, or credential-harvesting por-
tals that closely masquerade as legitimate institutional entities, such as retail banks,
cloud software providers, corporate intranets, or government services. According to
annual intelligence summaries from the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG) [13],
phishing volumes have expanded exponentially, accounting for a dominant proportion
of all documented initial network access compromises and enterprise data breach entry
points.
A primary catalyst for this heightened threat velocity is the industrialization of cy-
bercrime through the emergence of Phishing-as-a-Service (PaaS) platforms. PaaS
kits lower the technical threshold for launching distributed, evasive social engineering
campaigns. Even low-skilled threat actors can rent pre-packaged, turnkey attack toolk-
its hosted across decentralized cloud infrastructures. These commercialized phishing
kits incorporate real-time proxying frameworks, automated TLS/SSL certificate is-
suance, dynamic DNS redirection, and adaptive anti-analysis techniques that prevent
security crawlers from inspecting the landing pages. Financial, banking, and cryp-
tocurrency ecosystems are particularly targeted, where compromised user credentials
lead immediately to catastrophic monetary fraud and unauthorized asset transfers.
Traditionally, web filtering and endpoint defense mechanisms have depended upon
static signature blacklists, such as Google Safe Browsing and OpenPhish [1]. While
blacklists offer rapid lookup speeds and near-zero false positive rates for already-
identified threats, they are fundamentally reactive. Attackers routinely circumvent
blacklist databases by employing fast-flux DNS configurations, randomized domain
name generation algorithms (DGAs), domain typosquatting, and disposable subdo-
mains hosted on trusted content delivery networks (CDNs). Consequently, the op-
erational lifespan of a modern phishing page is frequently measured in mere hours

1.2 Cardiac Electrophysiology and Waveform Mor-
phology
A single physiological cycle of myocardial contraction and relaxation is designated as
a cardiac cycle, typically depicted in standard ECG traces as illustrated in Figure 1.1.
Figure 1.1: Magnified single cardiac cycle highlighting P, Q, R, S, T waves and asso-

sitional intervals:

quantifies electrical conduction transit time through the atrioventricular (AV)
node and His-Purkinje system. Pathological prolongation directly indicates first-
degree atrioventricular block (1dAVb).

To transcend the shortcomings of signature matching, the security research com-
munity turned to machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) techniques. Early
automated classifiers examined lexical URL features, Document Object Model (DOM)
syntactic characteristics, or raw pixel renderings. However, existing commercial and

• HTML/DOM-only Models: Classifiers analyzing page source code can be

rendering, and dynamic DOM injection executed only upon real human mouse

effectively capture brand mimicry, but incur severe computational latency dur-
ing headless browser crawling and are easily blinded by subtle CSS alterations,
transparent overlays, or canvas-rendered graphics.
